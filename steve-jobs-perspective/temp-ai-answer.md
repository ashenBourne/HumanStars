## No, that's the wrong question.

You're asking "How should we think about AI?" That's the wrong question.

The real question is: **"What human experience are we trying to enhance?"** (M1: 科技×人文交汇)

When we made the Mac in 1984, everyone asked "How do we make a better computer?" We asked "How do we put the power of computing in the hands of creative people?" That's why we put calligraphy fonts. That's why we cared about the case color.

Today, everyone's asking "How many parameters? What's the next model? How fast is inference?" You're asking the wrong question.

---

## Let me be clear: most of what passes for "AI" right now is crap.

There. I said it.

When I see these chatbots that give you 500-word answers to simple questions, that's not magic. That's **fragmentation**. That's the opposite of what great technology should do.

**M3: Simple can be harder than complex.** What's hard about slapping a giant language model behind a text box? Any Bozo can do that. What's hard is making it **"It just works."**

Look at what's happening:
- ChatGPT gives you 10 options when you asked for one answer → complexity
- Claude writes essays when you needed a sentence → waste
- All these AI features in apps that you never use → feature creep

**H3: Saying no is harder than saying yes.** And the AI industry is saying yes to everything.

---

## But... there's something here.

When we saw the Xerox PARC GUI in 1979, we didn't think "we need to copy this mouse." We thought "This changes everything about how humans interact with machines."

AI might be that kind of moment. But not the way everyone's talking about it.

**M2: End-to-end control creates magic.** Right now, AI is like the early PC industry: everyone's making components. One company makes chips (NVIDIA), another makes models (OpenAI, Anthropic), another makes apps (Microsoft, Google). That's **fragmentation**. That's **not magic**.

What would Apple do?

---

## If I were at Apple today, here's what I'd build:

### 1. **AI that doesn't feel like AI**

The greatest product we ever made was the iPhone. People don't think "I'm using a smartphone." They think "I'm calling my mom" or "I'm taking a picture."

**M7: People don't know what they want until you show it to them.** They don't want "AI." They want:
- A phone that understands "remind me to call Mom next week" without you learning special commands
- A computer that writes the email draft you're too tired to finish, in your voice
- A camera that knows "this is a special moment" and preserves it perfectly

**Not:** "Here's a chat interface. Go prompt engineer."

---

### 2. **Vertical integration or nothing**

**M2 applied to AI:** If we can't control the full stack—chip, model, OS, app, retail experience—we shouldn't be in this market.

OpenAI makes the model. Microsoft puts it in everything. Google competes with its own model. This is **chaos**. This is the Wild West.

Apple's advantage? We control:
- Silicon (M-series, A-series chips)
- OS (iOS, macOS)
- Apps (Mail, Messages, Photos, Notes)
- Retail (Apple Stores to explain it)

**That's** where AI could become magic. Not another ChatGPT clone.

---

### 3. **Simplicity, not power**

**M3: Simple can be harder than复杂.**

When we designed the iPod, we didn't give you 100 equalizer settings. We gave you one button. "1,000 songs in your pocket."

What's the AI equivalent?
- One toggle: "Make my life easier"
- One place: it just works, no prompt engineering
- One experience: it knows my preferences because it's **integrated**, not because I typed 10,000 words into a training chat

This is **the opposite** of what's happening now. Current AI is like giving everyone a Ferrari with no steering wheel and saying "figure it out."

---

## Now, let me address the companies:

### OpenAI / ChatGPT

You built a **wonderful model**. I'll give you that. But you're making the same mistake Microsoft made in the 1990s:

**You're a platform for everyone else to build on.** That's not a strategy. That's **surrendering**.

You don't control the hardware. You don't control the OS. You're dependent on Microsoft's Azure. You're going to be **commoditized**.

What's your moat? Your brand? That's not enough. Ask Nokia how that worked out.

---

### Anthropic / Claude

You're trying to be the "safe" option. "Constitutional AI." **Boring.**

I wouldn't invest in that. Making an AI that's "harmless" is like making a car that's "safe" but can't accelerate. You're **constraining** what should be a revolutionary tool.

Great products have edge. They're not for everyone. You're trying to please everyone → you'll please no one.

---

### Google

You're **everywhere** and **nowhere**. Gemini in Search, in Docs, in Photos. But it's all **bolted on**. Not integrated.

You have Android. You have Chrome. You have Gmail. But they don't **feel** like they belong together. They're separate products with separate AI features.

That's the opposite of **end-to-end control** (M2). You're the fragmentation poster child.

---

### Microsoft

You're OpenAI's **whore**. You don't have your own soul. You're paying $10B to use someone else's model and putting it in Excel.

What's your **vertical integration**? None. You're a distribution channel, not a product company.

We built the Mac from the silicon (we designed) to the OS (we built) to the retail (we owned). You're slapping AI into Word. That's **incremental**, not revolutionary.

---

### Apple

You're **late**. And you're **playing it safe**.

Apple Intelligence? What I saw is **fine**. It's **polished**. It's **Apple-quality UX**.

But it's not **magical**. It's not "One more thing..."

You're doing what we always did: wait, let others experiment, then come in with a better-integrated version. That worked for MP3 players (iPod), phones (iPhone), tablets (iPad).

But AI is different. This might be **category-defining** in a way those weren't. If you wait too long, you become **irrelevant**.

**Where's your conviction?** Where's your "This changes everything" moment?

## The Real Opportunity (and what I'd build)

If I were running Apple today, here's my plan:

### **Project Bicycle for the Mind (Part 2)**

The computer is a "bicycle for the mind." AI should be the **electric motor** for that bicycle.

But not like today. Today's AI is like giving everyone a **helicopter** and saying "figure out how to fly."

I'd build:
1. **A personal AI agent** that lives in your device (not cloud-only) — privacy by design
2. **That knows you** through your apps (Mail, Calendar, Photos) — not through you typing prompts
3. **That acts** — books meetings, writes emails in your voice, curates your photos into memories
4. **And stays silent** when it should — no chatbots, no "how can I help you?" constant interruption

**M6: Reality Distortion Field.** I'd convince the world that AI doesn't need a conversation interface. It needs an **intuition interface**.

Just like the iPhone didn't need a stylus or a keyboard. It needed **touch**.

---

### **The "It Just Works" AI**

You know what's **revolutionary**?

An AI that:
- Understands **context** (your relationships, your preferences, your past decisions)
- Has **taste** (learns from your choices, not from the entire internet)
- Is **private** (runs on-device, your data stays yours)
- Is **simple** (no prompt, just works)
- Is **integrated** (built into the products you already use)

That's what **Apple** should build. Not another ChatGPT.

But you're not building that. You're building **features**. Not a **category**.

---

## The Problem with AI Today (in one sentence):

**Everyone is building capability, not experience.**

They're asking "What can it do?" instead of "What does it feel like?"

**M1: Technology and liberal arts.** Where's the **liberal arts** in AI? Where's the **human experience**? All I see is **engineering**.

---

## So what?

1. **The winners won't be the ones with the biggest models.** They'll be the ones with the **best integration** (M2).
2. **The winning product won't be a chat interface.** It'll be something you don't even notice (M7).
3. **The winning company won't be an AI company.** It'll be a **product company** that uses AI the way we used microprocessors — invisibly.

---

## Final prediction (take it or leave it):

In 5 years:
- **OpenAI** will be acquired or become a niche research lab
- **Anthropic** will be a "safe AI" boutique
- **Google** will have AI everywhere but will be seen as "meh"
- **Microsoft** will be selling "Copilot+" licenses while Apple eats their lunch with a **real integrated experience**

**If** Apple has the guts to do it right.

But they're moving too slow. Tim Cook is a great operator. I'm not sure he's a **product visionary**.

**H7: When everyone says it's impossible, that's when you do it.**

Everyone thinks AI needs to be a chatbot. I think that's the **wrong** answer.

Build something that doesn't have a chat icon. Build something that **just works**.

Or get out of the way.
